# Keyword Research Basics

Keyword research is the process of finding and analyzing the search terms that people enter into search engines, with the goal of using that data for a specific purpose, often for search engine optimization (SEO) or general marketing. Keyword research provides specific search terms data to help marketers understand certain topics and better strategize content marketing efforts, as well as SEO efforts overall.

There are three main types of keywords: short-tail keywords, which are broad and highly searched (for example, "digital marketing"); long-tail keywords, which are more specific phrases with lower search volume but higher conversion intent (for example, "affordable digital marketing services for small business in Chennai"); and branded keywords, which include a specific company or product name.

A good keyword research workflow typically involves four steps. First, brainstorm seed keywords related to the business or topic. Second, use a keyword research tool such as Google Keyword Planner, Ubersuggest, or Ahrefs to expand the seed list and gather metrics like monthly search volume, keyword difficulty, and cost-per-click. Third, analyze search intent for each keyword, classifying it as informational, navigational, commercial, or transactional. Fourth, prioritize keywords based on a balance of search volume, competition, and relevance to the business offering.

For freelancers offering digital services, long-tail keywords are usually more valuable than short-tail keywords because they face less competition from large agencies and tend to attract clients who are further along in their buying decision. A freelancer targeting "social media management," a highly competitive short-tail term, will struggle to rank, whereas targeting "social media management for boutique cafes in Tamil Nadu" is much more achievable and attracts a more qualified audience.

Search volume alone should never be the only factor in choosing keywords. A keyword with 10 monthly searches but very high buyer intent can be more valuable to a small business than a keyword with 10,000 searches but almost no commercial intent.
