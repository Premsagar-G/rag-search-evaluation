# Content Strategy for SEO

Content is often described as the foundation of SEO because search engines ultimately exist to match user queries with the most useful, relevant piece of content available. A sound content strategy starts with mapping content to the stages of the buyer journey: awareness, consideration, and decision.

Awareness-stage content answers broad informational questions and targets top-of-funnel keywords, such as blog posts explaining "what is search engine optimization." Consideration-stage content compares options and solutions, such as "SEO agency vs freelance SEO consultant." Decision-stage content is directly commercial, such as service pages and case studies designed to convert a visitor into a client.

Content freshness matters for topics where information changes quickly, such as software tools, pricing, or algorithm updates. Search engines tend to favor recently updated content for these query types, so periodically refreshing older posts with current data can recover lost rankings.

Content length should be driven by user intent and topic complexity rather than an arbitrary word count target. A quick factual query may be best answered in 300 words, while a comprehensive guide to a complex topic may reasonably run several thousand words. What matters most is thoroughness relative to competing pages, not length for its own sake.

E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness) is a framework Google's quality raters use to assess content quality, and it is especially emphasized for topics that could affect a person's health, finances, or safety. Freelancers can strengthen E-E-A-T signals by including author bios, citing credible sources, and showcasing real client results.

A realistic content calendar for a freelancer managing a single client typically includes two to four blog posts per month, supplemented by ongoing updates to core service pages.
